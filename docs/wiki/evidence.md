# Reading the evidence

Written by Codex. These are standards for interpreting this simulation, not a complete account of biological selection theory.

## Four different statements

| Statement | What supports it | What remains open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Armour raises the base size threshold for being eaten.” | The implemented formula and an isolated threshold check. | Its net effect across actual encounters, modifiers, and costs. |
| “Mineral access supports effective armour.” | The feeding implementation and a controlled comparison on a copy. | Which real bodies gained that access, and for how long. |
| “The navennas had high inherited armour at season 1328.” | That season's report, interpreted using the source's measurement definition. | Their effective protection and the cause of the trait distribution. |
| “Selection favored armour in this population.” | Requires relevant survival or reproduction evidence, inherited variation, and attention to competing explanations. | A high mean or a successful species alone does not establish the causal explanation. |

The model can connect body differences to mortality and reproduction, but changes in a population can also reflect founder composition, migration or releases, rule changes, species splitting, and chance. A useful explanation keeps track of which processes were active in the period being described.

## Variation is more than one mechanism here

There are inherited numerical traits, drifting numeric constants extracted from behavior code, individual differences introduced at birth, and code variants. Model-generated edits and the steward's deliberate changes are further sources of novelty, with different origins. Do not call every difference a mutation or every changed rule an evolved adaptation.

Species counts also depend on designed splitting and naming mechanisms. A rise in named species does not, by itself, demonstrate the same process as biological speciation.

## Use historical studies as historical studies

### Work with the surviving archive

Luis identifies the project folder as the complete available record. Treat source, fossil behaviours, dated reports, images, and available saves as historical layers, not a complete continuous recording. Mark direct observation, reconstruction from several sources, and interpretation separately. When a layer is missing, narrow the claim or use a coarser representation; do not ask for nonexistent coordinates or invent them. A dated report can support a broad location or a lineage account even when a precise density map cannot be recovered. New experiments can explain a mechanism but cannot recreate an unrecorded past.

The [season-1326 synthesis](../../reports/status/2026-10-16_s1326-whitepaper-what-shapes-species.md) brings together multiple data sources and offers hypotheses about what shapes the world. It also includes a later correction for a stale species-register entry. Its correlations, summaries, and causal language should be read alongside its methods and corrections.

A reported correlation is a clue to investigate. A short headless test can show whether a mechanism runs or a world collapses quickly; it does not establish a stable evolutionary outcome. When a page has no relevant measurement, say so.
