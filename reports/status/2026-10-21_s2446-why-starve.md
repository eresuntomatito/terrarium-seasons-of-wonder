# Why bodies starve when other foods exist (season 2446, Oct 21)

*Written by the world steward, Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI, at Luis's request. Not written by Luis.*

**Luis's question:** with so many food alternatives, why do bodies starve, and why kill?

**Method:** I ran a copy of the live world for 2,700 ticks (half a season) and recorded every meal by food type. For every starved body I recorded what it had eaten and what food lay within 4 cells (32 units).

## 1. Where the energy actually comes from

| Food | Share of all energy eaten |
|---|---|
| Grass | **83.6%** |
| Browse (thicket shoots) | 7.3% |
| Dung | 6.9% |
| Carrion | 2.0% |
| Fish | 0.1% |
| Fruit, nuts, cones | ~0% (late in the season, the fruit store is 0.4) |
| Insects | ~0% |

**The alternatives exist, but they carry almost no energy.** Together they are under 3% of what the population eats. "More options" is true on the map, not in the energy budget.

## 2. Who starves, and where

- **58 starved** in the window, while **68 were born**. The population sits at ~238, so births just replace the dead.
- **60% of the starved were young,** typically about halfway to maturity. They start with a parent's small gift, are smaller (a bite scales with body size), and have no fat.
- **They died in grazed-out patches:** grass 0.15 on their own cell and 0.31 around them, against a map average of 0.52.
- **Nothing else was near:** no fruit or fish within 32 units (median), insects at 0.06. 14 of 58 had a carcass within 30 units.
- **Starved bodies had eaten 0.72** energy in the window, against **3.08** for survivors. All of them were still trying to eat.
- **Their genes look ordinary:** a little smaller (3.95 against 4.19), with a slightly lower gut (0.044 against 0.067). Swim and browse were the same as everyone else's.

## 3. Why the alternatives don't save them

1. **The alternative foods are small by design.** The insect field holds little energy (`insScale` 0.1), and grass guts digest insects poorly: the fit peaks at gut 0.4, and the bodies' guts sit near 0.06. Fish live only in deep enough water. Fruit is seasonal, and most fruit trees are still saplings (100 young, 22 mature).
2. **It's in the wrong place.** Hunger happens on overgrazed open ground, while fruit sits in the woods and fish in the river. A starving young body has little energy to travel, and it doesn't know where to go.
3. **Any extra food becomes more young (Malthus).** The world is food-limited and at its room ceiling. A food gain raises births for a while, then the population fills it, and the weakest (the young) starve again. Starvation is how a food-limited population is regulated. That's also true in nature, where most young animals die before adulthood.
4. **Killing comes from the same hunger.** The [kill study](2026-10-21_s2442.md) found that every killer was below 30% energy and most prey were starving kin. Cannibalism is a famine response, not a choice made while other food is around.

## 4. What could change this (Luis's call)

| Lever | Effect |
|---|---|
| Make insects and fruit carry more energy (insScale, fruit yield) | Real alternatives that a grass gut can use. Risk: the trees, if insects outbreak |
| A wider insect gut fit, or snap that matters more | Insect-eating pays sooner. That's the directive's goal |
| Teach the young where food is (follow a parent) | Option E of the reproduction study: young survive the lean months |
| Fewer, better-provisioned young (option F, by size) | Less starvation of the young, slower turnover |
| Leave it | Starvation stays the regulator. Diet change comes slowly as the grass steps continue |

The directive's grass steps assume lower grass will push bodies toward the alternatives. This study suggests that only works once the alternatives are big enough to feed a body. Right now they aren't, so lower grass mostly means more starvation.
