# Proposal: a bit of geology (season 1542, Oct 17)

**From Luis (s1536):** "we need deposition, and we need to explore how to introduce a bit of geology." Deposition is built and live (s1542). This page sets out what geology could add on top of it, how each piece would tie into what the world already does, and the questions to settle. Nothing below is built.

## What the land does today
- **Shape:** the relief (peak, meseta, saddle, and from s1406 the sections' frame), moved by **earthquakes** (faults lift and drop, and cliffs on the break fall into scree).
- **Water** routes downhill, **carves** its bed (up to 0.30 deep) and, **from s1542, deposits** what it carries where it slows, ponds or dries. Material is conserved, so lakes and deep cuts now refill.
- **Rock is all the same.** Every cell erodes at the same rate, and nothing differs underground. Reef is the only other "material": built by the dead, worn by water into fertile soil.
- **Minerals already matter to life:** shells are built from minerals (reef nearby, plus mineral in the soil).

## What geology could add (four pieces)

### 1. Rock that differs: hardness and layers (the core)
Each cell gets a **bedrock type** from a few kinds, for example:
- **hard** (granite-like): slow to erode, poor soil;
- **soft** (sandstone/shale-like): fast to erode, middling soil;
- **soluble** (limestone-like): dissolved by water, rich in minerals.

The types lie in **layers by depth**. A carved or faulted cell exposes the layer below. Water erodes at the rate of the exposed rock.
- **What emerges:**
  - **differential erosion**: hard caprock keeps the meseta flat-topped while soft layers beneath retreat into cliffs;
  - buttes and arches of scenery;
  - waterfalls where a river crosses from hard to soft rock;
  - canyons that widen in soft bands.
- **With the sections:** barriers of soft rock would slowly wear through, so passes open by geology rather than by rule. Hard-rock barriers would last.

### 2. Soils from rock and river (ties geology to life)
- **Soil quality follows the parent rock:**
  - sandy soils over soft rock drain fast (dry grass);
  - clay over shale holds water;
  - limestone soils carry **minerals**.
- **Deposited sediment becomes alluvium:** fertile, moist river plains and deltas where the water drops its load. Today deposition only changes the height; this would make the floodplains the richest grazing.
- **Minerals by rock:** shell-building already needs minerals. Limestone country would grow better shells, so shells would become **regional** (by section and by rock), and a herd might travel to a **salt lick**.

### 3. Slopes that fail: the angle of repose
- Ground steeper than its rock can hold **slumps slowly into scree** at the foot. Hard rock holds steeper, soft rock less.
- **What emerges:** cliffs retreat, talus slopes form at their feet, gullies widen, and the land stops holding impossible walls. Quakes already do this along faults; this would make it everyday.
- **With the sections:** the barriers' faces would soften over many seasons, unless they're hard rock. That's one more slow, natural pass.

### 4. Slow uplift: the land renews itself
- Without uplift, erosion and quakes only rearrange the land, and over very long times everything wears flat. A **slow uplift** under the range (a tiny rise per season, more where quakes have struck) keeps mountains standing against erosion.
- **What emerges:** a living balance between rising and wearing. The meseta's ribs deepen while the peak keeps its height.

## How they fit together

| Piece | Builds on | Changes for life | Cost (speed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Rock and layers | carving, quakes | where cliffs, canyons and passes form | small (a lookup per carve) |
| 2. Soils and minerals | deposition, fertility, moisture, shells | rich floodplains; **regional shells**; salt licks | small |
| 3. Slopes fail | relief, quakes' scree | cliffs soften and retreat; **barriers age** | moderate (a pass over steep cells every few updates) |
| 4. Uplift | relief, quakes | mountains endure | tiny |

## My recommendation
**Start with 1 + 2: rock with layers, and soils and minerals from it.** Together they make geology *matter to life*:
- floodplains become the best grazing;
- minerals and shells become regional;
- barriers wear according to their rock.

That's the same principle as the sections: shape the conditions, let evolution answer. Then add **3** once we've seen how the rock behaves, because it ages the sections' barriers, which you'd want to watch. **4** is cheap and can come any time, or when the range starts to look worn.

## Questions to settle
1. **Which pieces, and in what order?** (recommended: 1 + 2 first, then 3, then 4)
2. **How many rock kinds?** Three (hard, soft, soluble) is enough to see the effects; more is mostly scenery.
3. **How are the rocks laid out?**
   - (a) **flat layers by depth** everywhere (simple; the meseta's caprock emerges);
   - (b) layers **tilted or folded** under the range (more varied cliffs);
   - (c) **patches** at the surface by noise, plus layers below (the most varied).

   Recommended: (b) or (c).
4. **Should the sections' barriers be hard rock** (they last) **or ordinary rock** (they wear, and passes open slowly)? Recommended: ordinary rock, with a hard spine on the crests, so they age slowly rather than not at all.
5. **The carving cap (0.30):** with deposition now refilling, the cap could be lifted so rivers cut true canyons where the rock is soft. Recommended: lift it together with piece 1.
6. **Visible in the viewer?** Rock type tinting the ground; alluvium darker and greener. That's viewer work, for later.

## Deposition: first results (headless, 12,000 ticks vs today's rules)
- **Material conserved** (what's carved = what's carried + what's laid down).
- **Net land loss:** +3.7 of total height under today's rules, about 0 with deposition.
- **Refilling:** cells at the carving cap fell from 1,660 to 1,071 as deep cuts and lakes refilled.
- **No harm:** same bodies and water, same cost per tick, 0 errors.
- **To watch:** crossings (123 vs 80 in the last test season, noisy), in case shallower channels let more animals across.
