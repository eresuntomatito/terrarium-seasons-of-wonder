# Checkpoint — room for an apex to emerge, after 10 seasons (season 1381, Oct 17)

**Question (Luis, s1366):** instead of bringing the kraken back, find the rules that would let an apex predator **emerge on its own**. Four rules went live at s1371:
1. **Growth after adulthood:** a well-fed adult keeps growing slowly, up to 1.5× its inherited size.
2. **Bites that crack shells:** armour protects less against a bigger or wider-mouthed attacker.
3. **Room for rare species:** a species under 10% of the family's mass may breed past the mass ceiling.
4. **An apex niche:** an animal whose meat comes mostly from other meat-eaters counts as an apex, and a group of 15 can split off.

This is the 10-season checkpoint, for discussion.

**Rule by rule:**
- **Growth after adulthood: it works, but for the wrong animals.**
  - **Who grows biggest:** the well-fed **grazers** (thuzulis and tythuushs, gut 0.00–0.09). Grass is plentiful, so the grazers are the best fed.
  - **The family is a little bigger** (+11% grown).
  - **A side effect:** the thuzulis' **inherited** size fell (7.24 → 6.51). Bodies can now grow during life, so being born big pays less and selection lets the genes slide. Growth during life replaced growth by inheritance.
  - **The cost:** bigger bodies cost more, and when the grass dipped (s1374) the family fell from 229 to 189 before recovering.
- **Bites that crack shells: they work.** **Predation doubled** (≈27 → ≈59 kills per season), and the navennas were still eaten 213 times in 16 seasons despite armour at 0.98. The liralis, the main hunters, made **319 kills in 10 seasons** (+67%).
- **Room for rare species:** the liralis hovered around 9–16% of the family's mass, near the 10% line. I can't tell an effect apart from the rest yet.
- **The apex niche:** **no apex**, and no animal lives mainly on meat. Meat is still a side food (the highest gut is 0.27; a meat specialist needs about 0.5 or more), and there's nothing yet that hunts the hunters.

**Selection (s1365 → s1381):** fat rose (0.83 → 0.98), shells rose (0.64 → 0.72), gape rose a little (0.82 → 0.84), inherited size fell a little (6.90 → 6.71), gut didn't move. Starvation (1,105) still outweighs predation (851) as a cause of death.

## Reading
The four rules removed the **walls** (shells, room, recognition), but not the **direction**: nothing makes a hunter grow bigger than a grazer, and nothing pulls guts toward meat strongly enough. Growth went to whoever eats best, and in a grassy world that's the grazers.

## Options for discussion
1. **Growth fed by meat** (recommended): growth after adulthood is fuelled only, or mostly, by meat and carrion (protein), not by grass. Then only hunters get bigger, which is what lets a hunter outgrow other hunters. It also removes the side effect on the grazers' inherited size.
2. **Richer meat from meat-eaters** (from the original list): prey that are themselves meat-eaters give more energy. Hunting hunters pays, which is the apex niche's whole point.
3. **Slower lives for big bodies** (from the original list): bigger bodies mature later and have fewer young. This keeps an apex rare and stable, but only matters once one exists.
4. **Give it time:** gut is the slow step, and 10 seasons (≈6 generations) is short. Keep watching to s1400 and decide then.

My suggestion: **1 + 2 together**, then watch for 20 seasons. Option 1 steers growth to hunters, and option 2 makes the top of the chain pay.
