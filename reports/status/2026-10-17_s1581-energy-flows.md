# Report: how energy flows in this world (season 1581, Oct 17)

**Asked by Luis (s1581):** "a report on the current state of how energy flows in this world, and what gaps or where it is stuck, conservation of energy blocked; energy cycles not supported by the current ecology, and what could be supported but not explored — with a flow map and a list of recommendations."

**Method:** a new observing tool, `tools/energy-ledger.mjs`, ran one season (5,400 ticks) on a copy of the live world and counted every store and flow in body-energy units. It doesn't change the simulation. Plants are counted at a pure grazer's gut and average grass quality. Living costs are what remains once income, change in stores and deaths are known. The season was **dry**, so the plant store drew down.

## 3. Where energy is stuck, or conservation is broken

1. **Energy from nothing: the dung gift.** A body with decomposer ability is paid a fixed amount whenever a grazer of another species is near. It's the largest flow in the world: three times the sun's input. The world's 219 animals are mostly running on it. It is also why the dung lines are many, shelled, and live beside each other.
2. **Undigested plant matter is wasted as energy.** Grazers eat 1,519 and digest 1,053. The 704 they don't digest leaves as dung, but only as *fertility* (a nutrient). This is the real dung energy that nature's dung-eaters live on, and the world doesn't let anyone eat it.
3. **Kills vanish.** 77% of a kill's energy disappears before any body or carcass gets it: only 35% is kept by half-way guts, and the meat is shared. Only 15% falls as scraps. In nature, what a hunter can't eat stays on the carcass for scavengers and decomposers.
4. **Prey are nearly empty.** A body's own tissue holds almost no energy (29 across all 240 bodies), because tissue only builds slowly as a body grows. A kill yields mostly the prey's working energy plus fat, about 1.3 per kill on average. Size doesn't make prey a bigger meal, so hunting can't pay however good the hunter.
5. **Carcasses mostly rot.** Of 86 in, 40 is eaten as carrion and the rest goes straight to soil. There is no decomposer chain on carcasses beyond a gut bonus for carrion.
6. **Grazing outran growth** in this dry season (1,519 eaten vs 664 grown). The plant store absorbs it now, but it's what the leaner-grass rule was meant to stop: food, not room, setting the numbers.

## 4. Cycles today

| Cycle | Supported? | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Nutrients:** soil → plants → dung, withering, rot → soil | **yes, closed** | fertility feeds growth (growth ∝ moisture + fertility) |
| **Minerals:** rot → mineral → reef → water wear → soil; shells built from minerals | **yes, mostly** | shells' minerals aren't returned at death (open) |
| **Energy: sun → plants → grazers → hunters → carcasses → scavengers** | **barely** | flesh 0.1% and carrion 1.3% of income |
| **Energy: plants → grazers → dung → decomposers** | **broken** | the decomposers' energy doesn't come from the dung |
| **Energy: carcasses → decomposers → soil** | **short-circuited** | carcasses rot directly to soil |
| **Energy: decomposers → hunters** | **blocked** | the dung lines carry shells of 0.95 |

## 5. Cycles the world could support but doesn't explore

- **A real detritus chain.** Undigested plant matter (704/season) and kill remains (~40/season) as food for decomposers. In real ecosystems most energy moves this way, not through predators.
- **Decomposers as prey.** Small, soft dung-eaters fed on by hunters: plants → grazers → dung → decomposers → hunters → carcasses → decomposers. This closes the loop through the meat side.
- **Bodies as meals.** If tissue held the energy it cost to build, big prey would be worth big risks, and size would matter on both sides.
- **Eggs and the young as food.** Juveniles are already eaten (the eaten nanasas averaged size 2.8). An egg stage is only used by the kraken.
- **Water's own chain.** Water plants feed waders and swimmers, but nothing eats swimmers in the water. The alluvium and river plains enrich only grass.
- **Seasonal storage.** Fat carries energy through cold eras and droughts. Lichen, buds and carcasses (which keep in the cold) are seasonal foods. A body that migrates up to the heights in winter isn't yet a way of life.
- **Minerals returned.** Shells' minerals could return to the soil at death (limestone country, reef), closing the mineral loop.

## 6. Recommendations (in order)

1. **Honest dung, sourced from what grazers don't digest.** The dung load should carry the energy the grazer didn't extract (about 700 a season here), and decomposers eat from it until it's gone. *Caution:* removing the free 2,044 at once would cut income by two-thirds and starve the world. Done this way, about a third remains and the rest has to come from real food. Go live with a gradual rollout (like the leaner grass), and watch the dung lines. *(Honest dung is being implemented and tested now. Its source should be checked against this before it goes live.)*
2. **What a kill doesn't feed stays on the carcass.** The share no diner keeps goes to the carcass instead of vanishing. Scavengers and decomposers then get the hunter's leftovers, and carrion stops being a trickle.
3. **Carcasses feed decomposers before they rot:** decomposer ability gives access to carcasses (not only a carrion-gut bonus), and rot slows where decomposers are eating. Energy then cycles through bodies before reaching the soil.
4. **Bodies hold the energy they cost.** Building tissue at birth and while growing costs energy, and the tissue gives it back at death. Prey become real meals in proportion to size, and big bodies become prizes and risks.
5. **Then the roles.** With a sourced detritus chain and full carcasses, revisit what traits and costs make each role pay (your next topic): shells' upkeep and minerals, the gut valley, decomposers as prey, the apex options.

All of these keep to "physics first": energy enters only through plants, is lost only to living, and otherwise moves between bodies, carcasses, dung and soil.

## 7. Caveats
- **One dry season.** A wet season would show more growth and less drawdown; the shares (dung 65%, flesh 0.1%) are structural and won't change much. The ledger can be rerun any time: `node tools/energy-ledger.mjs [world] [ticks]`.
- **Living costs are a residual:** income minus change in stores minus deaths. They lump metabolism, movement, climbing, cold, wading, falls, lunges, birth losses and fat-making losses.
