# Why no split, drift or divide? (season 2235, Oct 21)

*Written by the world steward, Claude (Opus 5.5), an AI, at Luis's request. Not written by Luis.*

**The question (Luis):** why has the world shown no new species — no split, drift or divide — since the quilikes became the only species at s1853, about 380 seasons ago?

**Method:**
- On a private copy of the live save (s2235, 240 bodies), I ran each speciation route exactly as the world runs it, but recording instead of splitting.
- I measured how far apart the bodies are, and how different natural groups are from random groups.
- The world's own split counters couldn't be used: they are **not saved** with the world, so every restart wiped them (§6).

## The short answer
**One route is trying every day and failing narrowly, one route is switched off, and the other two never get started.**
- **The niche split is the one that tries:** the highland band, 22 founders. Its body difference falls short of the bar the rules set, and part of the reason is that the test **doesn't look at swimming or climbing at all**. Those are the traits physics now selects hardest on.
- **The great divide is switched off** by design (s1847).
- **Drift and mind splits** find nothing to work with: everyone can still mate with nearly everyone, and the mind variants have only 1–3 carriers.

## 1. The great divide: switched off on purpose
The divide (splitting a species by place) was cancelled when `speciate2` went live at s1847, at Luis's direction (s1837: "we want species that are practically different, not just renamed"). A probe showed its pieces differed by **0.20 SD** in body, while random halves differed by **0.19**: they were renamed copies, told apart only by where they lived. So "no divide" is the rule working as intended.

## 2. The niche split: tries every day, blocked by the body test
- **The highland band** (33 bodies now, 22 gathered in one place) has been flagged as "living differently" on **39 daily checks in a row**. It passes the count gates (≥ 15 founders, ≥ 30 left behind) every time.
- **Then it meets the body test.** The founders' body traits must differ from the rest by **0.5 SD more than random founders of the same size would**.
  - The highland band scores **0.412** against a random **0.21**: it really is twice as different as chance.
  - But the bar is 0.71, so it's blocked, 30 out of 30 tries in my run.

**The test looks at the wrong traits for this world.** It compares 11 body traits:
- size, gut, armour, toxin, legs, wind, maturity, browse, gape, resistance, litter.

It does *not* include:
- **swim, climb, bite, snap, coat or decomp.**

Those are exactly the traits the newest rules select on (M2 physics, the river, the heights, insects). A band that has become a much better climber or swimmer looks, to the test, like everybody else. Even the averaging works against it: one or two very different traits get diluted across eleven.

## 3. The drift split: everyone can still mate with everyone
- **How mating works:** two bodies may mate only while their kin gap (code, body and genes) is under **0.2**.
- **Measured over all 28,680 pairs alive today:**

  | | Gap |
  |---|---|
  | Median | 0.168 |
  | p90 | 0.201 |
  | Maximum | 0.265 |

  **Only 10% of pairs are past the line**, and every one of those can still mate with the other 90%, so genes flow through them.
- **What the drift split needs:** two groups so different that their *centres* sit 0.2 apart. With one connected gene pool, that never happens, so the drift check stops before it even reaches the body test.

## 4. The mind split: not enough carriers
- **The variants:** two "new ways of thinking" exist, carried by 3 and 1 bodies.
- **The bar:** a mind split needs 15 carriers gathered in one place.
- **Why they stay rare:** new minds appear often (the chronicle reports them), but none has spread, because none pays.

## 5. The deeper reason: the same pressures everywhere, and no cost to having it all
- **The regions barely differ.** For every trait, the spread between the four lowland regions is tiny next to the spread within them (between/within):

  | Trait | Between/within |
  |---|---|
  | swim | 0.004 |
  | climb | 0.022 |
  | size | 0.009 |
  | browse | 0.031 |

  So selection pushes the same way everywhere. Bodies stay ~92 units from home, so it isn't mixing; it's that **every region asks for the same body**.
- **The bodies differ very little from each other.** The relative spread of most traits is 7–15% (gut is the exception), so there's little raw material to split along.
- **Every body gains every skill at once.** Climb, swim, browse and gut all rose together in the last windows (reports s2151, s2189). Holding many skills costs nothing extra, so there's no pressure to become a specialist. Without specialists there are no "practically different" groups, and that's what Luis asked species to be. **This is Luis's skills-cost idea** (s2101): if being good at one thing makes you worse at another, bands that live differently will also *be* different.

## 6. A small bookkeeping gap
The split counters (`world.speciate`: attempts, blocks, the gaps measured) are not written into the save. Every restart, and there were nine on Oct 20, erased the record of what was tried. **The steward proposes saving them,** which changes nothing in the world's behaviour, so future reports can show the attempts.

## 7. What could change it (for Luis to decide; nothing is built)

| Option | What it does | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Skills cost** | A trade-off between abilities (climb, swim, browse, snap, gut…), so specialists win their niche and generalists pay. The root cause in §5 | New rule (Luis's idea, already on the backlog) |
| **B. Count the new traits** | Add swim, climb, bite, snap and coat to the body test. The highland band and the swimmers would then be measured on what makes them different | Rule fix, small |
| **C. Judge the biggest differences, not the average** | Score a group on its 2–3 most different traits (in SD) instead of the RMS of eleven. A band that's very different in a few traits counts | Rule fix, small |
| **D. Lower the bar** | `speciateGap` 0.5 → ~0.25 would let the highland band (and the biggest bodies) split today | Parameter, but risks the "renamed copies" Luis didn't want |
| **E. Like mates like, by way of life** | Bodies prefer mates that live the same way (highland with highland), which slows gene flow between bands | New rule |
| **F. Save the split counters** | Bookkeeping only (§6) | Steward can do it now |

**The steward's recommendation:** do **F** now. Then **B** (or B + C) together with **A** when Luis is ready to talk about skills cost. B alone might give a highland species soon, but without A it would likely blend back into the main population. A gives the split a reason to last.
