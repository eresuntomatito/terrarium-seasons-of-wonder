# Proposal — four isolated sections (season 1375, Oct 16)

**From Luis' original orography idea:** the peak, the plateau ("meseta" in Spanish; *plateau* or *tableland* in English) and the river were meant to cut the world into **four sections**. Today they don't. This page explains why and sets out the options and the questions to settle. Nothing has been built.

## Why there are no four sections today

The map **wraps both ways** (leave the right edge and you come back at the left; the same for top and bottom), so geometrically it's a **torus**, a doughnut. On a torus:

- **The range** (peak + plateau) cuts the map horizontally, but the lowlands above and below it are **joined through the top/bottom seam**: they're one lowland, and the range is a strip inside it.
- **The river** cuts that lowland vertically, but east and west are **joined through the left/right seam**: cutting a loop once leaves one piece.
- And neither is a hard barrier: the range has the canyon gap (and a small gap at the seam), and the river can be waded.

So everything is one connected place. (The [orography X-ray](2026-10-16_s1365.md) shows the shapes; the [whitepaper](2026-10-16_s1326-whitepaper-what-shapes-species.md) explains why isolation matters for new species.)

## Options

| | Option | Result | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **A second range and a second river** halfway round the doughnut: a range near the top (about y = 15%) and a river near x = 82% | 2 × 2 = 4 lowland sections, with the ranges as high country between them | new relief; a busier map |
| **B** *(recommended)* | **Put the second barriers on the seams:** a mountain wall along the top/bottom edge and a river along the left/right edge | **the map's frame becomes the border**: with the existing range and river, **4 sections as originally pictured** (north-west, north-east, south-west, south-east), unequal in size | cheap: the edges become terrain, and the wrapping physics stay as they are |
| **C** | **Stop the map wrapping** (hard edges) | 4 quadrants from one range and one river | large: movement, water and neighbours all assume wrapping; the river would need a source and a sink |

## What makes them sections: barrier strength
- **Ranges** today are only costly to climb. For isolation, the crest would need to be cliffs or bitter cold that few bodies can cross: perhaps impassable for most, crossable by thick-coated, long-legged climbers.
- **Rivers** today are only costly to wade. For isolation, the channel would need to be deep, crossable only by good swimmers. The swimming skill (which water plants have started to raise) would then decide who moves between sections.
- **Passes that open now and then**, so isolation isn't permanent:
  - a **frozen river** in cold eras (walk across the ice);
  - a **dry bed** in droughts;
  - a **low saddle** in the range;
  - **gaps opened by earthquakes**.

  Long separation with rare crossings is ideal for new species.

## Questions to settle (Luis)
1. **A, B or C?** (recommended: B)
2. **How hard are the barriers:** impassable for most, or crossable by specialists (climbers, swimmers)?
3. **Which passes, if any:** ice, drought, saddles, quakes?
4. **Should each section get its own character** (one wetter, one colder, one with more thicket), or differ only by chance and what evolves there?
