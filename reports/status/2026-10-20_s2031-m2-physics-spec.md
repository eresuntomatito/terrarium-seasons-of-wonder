## 1. Strength (derived, not inherited)
`strength(c) = (mass(size) / mass(5)) ** (2/3) × (0.5 + 0.5 × clamp01(c.energy / (capOf(c) × P.weakAt)))`
- `size = this.bodySize(c)`, and `mass` is the existing helper.
- Muscle scales with cross-section, and hunger weakens.
- Cache it per body per tick if it's used more than once.

## 2. Walking on slopes: Tobler
- With `physics` on, replace the `slope` factor in `steer()` (line ~2519) with Tobler's curve along the direction of travel:
  `tob = exp(−3.5 × |g + 0.05|) / exp(−3.5 × 0.05)`
  Here `g` is rise/run in metres: sample `elevAt` one step ahead along `(vx, vy)` minus here. Height 1.0 = 100 m and 1 unit = 0.65 m, so `g = Δelev × 100 / (step × 0.65)`.
- Normalise so the best (slight downhill) is 1. Clamp to ≥ 0.25.
- Long legs flatten it: `slope = 1 − (1 − tob) × clamp(1.3 − 0.5 × legs, 0.3, 1)`.
- Keep `climbCost` energy as it is.

## 3. Climbing takes time (climbing state)
Today `crossGate`/`canStep` treat crags and cliffs as a yes/no gate each step. With `physics` on:
- When a body passes the gate onto a cell with `need > P.needFloor` that's higher than its own (the existing `pass(...)`), it enters **`c.climbing = { left: ticks }`** instead of stepping freely.
- **Ticks** = `ceil(riseMetres / vUpPerTick)`:
  - `riseMetres = max(0, Δelev) × 100`, at least `P.climbMinRise` (2 m);
  - `vUpPerTick = 0.4 m/s × 0.65 s × climbSkill × strength`, where `climbSkill = c.climb`.
  - A body with climb ≤ the need doesn't pass at all; the existing gate already holds.
  - Cap at `P.climbMaxTicks` (240).
- **While climbing:**
  - the body moves at ≤ 15% of its limit, along its heading;
  - it doesn't eat, breed or sprint, and danger doesn't steer it (it can't flee);
  - it pays `climbCost` as now, plus `P.climbHold × mass ratio` per tick;
  - when `left` reaches 0, the state clears.
- **Coming down** a cell with need > needFloor (the next cell is lower): no climbing state, but there's a **fall risk** per such step of `P.downFall × (1 − climb) × need`. A fall reuses the existing fall branch in `crossGate` (move down the steepest way, `fallCost`, `this.fell++`).
- Count ticks spent climbing in `this.climbTicks` (for the test and reports).

## 4. Catching a climber (Luis's decision 5, with his condition)
A body in the climbing state **can be caught only by a hunter whose `climb` ≥ the prey's `climb`.**
- Find the predation/attack code (search `kills`, `'eaten'`, `bite`, `reach`). Where an attack would land on a climbing prey, refuse it unless `hunter.climb >= prey.climb`. That attacker then can't reach it, so it gets no kill and no energy.
- When the hunter qualifies, the prey's lack of fleeing already makes it easier to catch. Don't add a bonus.

## 5. Swimming is slower; skill and strength matter
- In swim-deep water (the same `deep > 1` test as the wading block, line ~2406), the speed limit in `steer()` gets the factor
  `swimFactor = (0.2 + 0.5 × swim) × sqrt(strength)`,
  capped at 1. It replaces the `wade` factor there; wading below swim depth is unchanged.
- Store the needed flag on the body (e.g. `c.swimming = deep > 1`), set where `c.wading` is set, so `steer()` can read it.
- Strength also resists the current: the sweep term `k` is multiplied by `1 / max(0.5, strength)`.
- Swim upkeep is unchanged.
